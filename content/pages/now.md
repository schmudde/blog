## Yorba: New Media

I'm still building [Yorba](https://yorba.co) with a small, committed team. The internet is very good at starting relationships and terrible at ending them, so this fall the work is about making automating cleanup. Automatic unsubscribe shipped at the end of the summer, and we're working on a few other tools to help you maintain your privacy.

Speaking of automatic unsubscribe, I spoke a little about the problem that keeps arriving in your inbox with *Forbes* in March 2026:

> David Schmudde, the CTO at Yorba commented on the trend: “We're currently in the Wild West of AI. Solutions have been proposed: charging senders fractions of a cent to send an email, providing humans with an immutable "heartbeat" identifier, but no one is experimenting with rolling them out. Until we get serious about this, I'm afraid the problem is just going to get worse.”

~ *[The Cost Of Convenience In The Age Of AI](https://www.forbes.com/sites/garydrenik/2026/03/05/the-cost-of-convenience-in-the-age-of-ai/)*

Underneath all of it is Eulogy, which I'm still working toward fully open-sourcing. Eulogy is a textual representation of the data locked into other people's computers. It uses a set of auditable claims to map your digital footprint and assert your legal right to privacy. It also carries the context that makes that data meaningful, so the record stays portable and can live on your own device, outliving the software that produced it. It began as a practical choice: as a bootstrapped company with local-first ambitions, we couldn't afford to gamble on new technology, so we built on popular open schemas and left the storage substrate to be resolved later. Making it public is how the record becomes yours rather than ours.

Chris (the CEO) and I write about this in [*Internet, Less.*](https://www.yorba.co/internet-less), our perspective on the state of the internet, where it's going, and what a quieter future could look like. My latest, "[Finding Your Place in an Unimaginably Large Space](https://www.yorba.co/internet-less/unimaginably-large)," looks at why the web's enormous scale concentrates around a few dominant platforms, what that top-heavy market means for a small subscription business like ours, and why we're choosing to grow through partnerships, members who spread the word, and shared data with researchers instead of louder promotion. It follows "[Escaping the Paradox of More](https://www.yorba.co/internet-less/escaping-the-paradox-of-more)."
